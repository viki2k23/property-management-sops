# Owner Communication and Reporting SOP

**Document type:** Standard Operating Procedure
**Category:** Leasing Operations — Owner Relations
**Applies to:** Property managers, operations coordinators, and remote
support staff managing communication with property owners and investors
**Last updated:** June 2026
**Author:** Vicky | Property Management Operations

---

## Purpose

This SOP establishes a consistent, professional, and proactive
communication framework for managing property owner relationships.
It defines what gets reported, when, how, and in what format —
ensuring owners are always informed, never surprised, and confident
that their investment is being managed with care.

Owner communication is the single most important relationship in
property management. A tenant who is unhappy calls. An owner who
is unhappy leaves — and takes their entire portfolio with them.

This SOP protects that relationship through structure, transparency,
and consistency.

---

## Scope

Applies to all communication with property owners including routine
reporting, financial updates, maintenance escalations, tenant issues,
vacancy updates, and lease decisions requiring owner approval.

---

## Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Owner** | The property owner or investor whose assets are being managed |
| **NTE** | Not-to-Exceed — the pre-approved spending threshold for repairs |
| **Owner approval** | Written confirmation from the owner before proceeding with a decision |
| **Monthly owner report** | A structured monthly summary of property performance sent to every owner |
| **Escalation** | Any situation requiring owner awareness or decision before action |
| **Reserve fund** | Funds held in trust for future repairs and maintenance |

---

## Communication Principles

Every owner communication must follow these principles:

**1. Proactive over reactive**
Never let an owner find out about a problem from a tenant, a neighbor,
or a code enforcement notice. If something significant happens at their
property, they hear it from you first — with context and a plan.

**2. Facts before opinions**
Report what happened, what the impact is, and what the options are.
Let the owner make decisions with complete information — do not
filter facts to make situations seem better or worse than they are.

**3. Solutions with problems**
Never present a problem without at least one recommended solution.
"The HVAC failed and I need your input" is weak. "The HVAC failed —
here are two repair options and my recommendation — I need your
approval to proceed" is professional.

**4. Written confirmation of all decisions**
Every owner decision must be confirmed in writing. If an owner
approves something verbally, follow up immediately with an email
confirmation. Verbal approvals that are not documented do not exist
operationally.

**5. Consistent reporting rhythm**
Owners should never need to chase you for updates. A monthly report
delivered on schedule — without the owner asking — is the foundation
of a trusted management relationship.

## Owner Communication Types

| Type | Trigger | Timeframe |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate escalation | Emergency, major incident, legal threat | Within 2 hours |
| Approval request | Decision requiring owner sign-off | Before taking action |
| Monthly report | End of each month | By the 5th of the following month |
| Vacancy update | Unit becomes vacant or tenant gives notice | Within 24 hours |
| Lease decision update | New lease, renewal, or non-renewal | Within 48 hours |
| Routine update | Non-urgent information owners should know | Weekly or as needed |

## Step-by-Step Process

### Step 1 — Immediate escalation protocol

Escalate to the owner immediately — within 2 hours — for any of
the following situations:

- Emergency maintenance with estimated cost above NTE threshold
- Fire, flood, or structural damage at the property
- Tenant threat of legal action or rent withholding
- Code enforcement inspection or violation notice received
- Confirmed bedbug or rodent infestation
- Tenant injury reported on the property
- Tenant abandonment or unauthorized occupant discovered
- Eviction filing required
- Insurance claim situation

**Immediate escalation template:**

> Hi [Owner Name],
>
> I am reaching out immediately regarding a situation at
> [Property Address] that requires your awareness.
>
> **What happened:**
> [Clear, factual description of the incident]
>
> **Current status:**
> [What has been done so far and current state of the situation]
>
> **Impact:**
> [Effect on the tenant, property, or finances]
>
> **Recommended next step:**
> [Your specific recommendation]
>
> **What I need from you:**
> [Decision or approval needed — be specific]
>
> **Timeframe:**
> [When you need a response to keep things moving]
>
> Please call or respond to this message as soon as possible.
> I am available at [phone number].
>
> [Your Name]
> [Company Name] — Property Management

---

### Step 2 — Approval request process

Obtain owner approval in writing before proceeding with any of
the following:

- Repair or maintenance cost exceeding the pre-approved NTE
- Any repair requiring permits or licensed contractor certification
- Lease terms that differ from the owner's stated preferences
- Accepting a tenant who does not fully meet screening criteria
- Non-renewal of a current tenant's lease
- Any legal action including eviction filing
- Changes to rental rate outside agreed parameters
- Use of reserve funds above a threshold amount

**Approval request template:**

> Hi [Owner Name],
>
> I need your approval before proceeding with the following:
>
> **Property:** [Address]
> **Situation:** [Brief description of what requires a decision]
>
> **Options:**
>
> Option 1: [Description]
> Estimated cost: $[Amount]
> Pros: [Key advantages]
> Cons: [Key disadvantages]
>
> Option 2: [Description]
> Estimated cost: $[Amount]
> Pros: [Key advantages]
> Cons: [Key disadvantages]
>
> **My recommendation:** Option [1/2] because [brief reason]
>
> **Please reply with your approval or preferred option by
> [date and time] so we can proceed without delay.**
>
> If I do not hear back by [deadline], I will [default action
> if applicable — or] hold until I receive your direction.
>
> [Your Name]
> [Company Name] — Property Management

**After receiving verbal approval — follow up immediately:**

> Hi [Owner Name],
>
> Thank you for your approval on our call today regarding
> [situation at Property Address].
>
> Confirming your approval to proceed with:
> [Specific action approved]
> Estimated cost: $[Amount]
>
> I will keep you updated as this progresses.
>
> [Your Name]

month — without exception and without waiting to be asked.

### Step 4 — Vacancy update

When a tenant gives notice or a unit becomes vacant, notify the
owner within 24 hours.

**Vacancy notification template:**

> Hi [Owner Name],
>
> I wanted to notify you promptly that [Tenant Name] at
> [Property Address, Unit #] has given notice to vacate.
>
> **Move-out date:** [Date]
> **Notice received:** [Date]
> **Notice compliant with lease:** Yes / No — [details if no]
>
> **Recommended next steps:**
>
> 1. Schedule move-out inspection for [proposed date]
> 2. Begin marketing the unit — proposed rent: $[Amount]
>    based on current market comps
> 3. Assess unit for any turnover repairs needed
>    Estimated turnover cost: $[Amount — rough estimate]
>
> I will begin the marketing process as soon as I have your
> confirmation on the proposed rent amount.
>
> Please let me know if you would like to discuss or if you
> have a different direction in mind.
>
> [Your Name]
> [Company Name] — Property Management

### Step 6 — Document and file all owner communications

Every owner communication must be filed and retained:

- [ ] All emails saved to owner's property file
- [ ] All verbal approvals followed up in writing and saved
- [ ] Monthly reports saved with date sent and confirmation
      of receipt where possible
- [ ] All approval requests saved with owner's response attached
- [ ] Escalation communications saved with outcome documented

**Retention period:** Minimum 5 years for all owner communications
related to financial decisions, legal matters, or significant
maintenance. Minimum 3 years for routine communications.

## Owner Communication Dos and Don'ts

**Do:**
- Always lead with facts — not emotions or opinions
- Always present a recommendation with every problem
- Always confirm verbal approvals in writing immediately
- Always notify before a situation escalates — not after
- Always send monthly reports on schedule without being asked
- Always give owners enough context to make informed decisions

**Do not:**
- Surprise an owner with a problem they should have heard about sooner
- Present a problem without a recommended solution
- Wait for an owner to ask for a monthly report
- Take action above the NTE threshold without written approval
- Share negative tenant information in a way that creates legal risk
- Make promises to owners about outcomes you cannot guarantee
