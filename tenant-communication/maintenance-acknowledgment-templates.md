# Maintenance Acknowledgment Templates

**Document type:** Communication Templates
**Category:** Tenant Communication — Maintenance
**Applies to:** Property managers, operations coordinators, and remote
support staff handling tenant maintenance communication for U.S.
residential properties
**Last updated:** June 2026
**Author:** Vicky | Property Management Operations

---

## Purpose

This document provides ready-to-use templates for acknowledging,
updating, and closing out tenant maintenance requests. Clear,
timely maintenance communication is one of the highest-impact
factors in tenant satisfaction and lease renewal rates.

Tenants do not expect perfection — they expect to be heard,
kept informed, and treated professionally. A tenant who receives
no communication after submitting a request assumes nothing is
happening. A tenant who receives a prompt acknowledgment and
regular updates stays calm, cooperative, and more likely to renew.

---

## Scope

Applies to all maintenance-related tenant communication from
initial request acknowledgment through repair completion and
work order close-out.

---

## When to Use Each Template

| Template | When to use |
|---|---|
| 1 — Initial acknowledgment | Immediately upon receiving any maintenance request |
| 2 — Emergency acknowledgment | Within 30 minutes of receiving an emergency request |
| 3 — Vendor scheduled notification | Once vendor appointment is confirmed |
| 4 — Repair delay notification | When scheduled repair cannot proceed as planned |
| 5 — Entry notice | Minimum 24 hours before entering the unit |
| 6 — Repair completed confirmation | After vendor confirms repair is done |
| 7 — Follow-up — issue unresolved | If tenant reports repair did not fix the problem |
| 8 — Work order close-out | Final confirmation when issue is fully resolved |
| 9 — Repair outside scope notice | When requested repair is tenant responsibility |
| 10 — Preventive maintenance notice | Before scheduled routine or preventive work |

## Template 1 — Initial Acknowledgment (Routine Request)

**Send within:** 1 business day of receiving request
**Channel:** Email or text

---

**Email version:**

**Subject:** Maintenance Request Received — [Property Address, Unit #]

> Hi [Tenant Name],
>
> Thank you for submitting your maintenance request. We have
> received your report regarding [brief description of issue]
> at [Property Address, Unit #] and have logged it in our system.
>
> **Request details:**
> - Issue reported: [Description]
> - Date received: [Date]
> - Work order number: [Number]
> - Priority level: Routine
>
> We will contact you within [2–3 business days] with next steps,
> including the scheduled date for a vendor visit.
>
> In the meantime:
> - Please do not attempt to repair the issue yourself
> - If the issue worsens or becomes urgent, contact us immediately
> - We may need access to your unit — we will provide at least
>   24 hours notice before any entry
>
> Thank you for bringing this to our attention.
>
> [Your Name]
> [Title]
> [Company Name] — Property Management
> [Phone] | [Email]

---

**Text version:**

> Hi [Tenant Name], this is [Your Name] from [Company Name].
> We received your maintenance request for [brief issue] at
> [Address]. Work order [#] has been created. We will follow
> up within [2–3 business days] with scheduling details.
> Contact us immediately if the issue gets worse. Thank you.

## Template 2 — Emergency Acknowledgment

**Send within:** 30 minutes of receiving emergency request
**Channel:** Phone call first — follow up with text and email

---

**Phone call opening:**

> Hi [Tenant Name], this is [Your Name] from [Company Name]
> calling about the emergency you reported at [Address].
> I have received your report and I am taking action right now.
> I am contacting our emergency vendor and will call you back
> within [30–60 minutes] with an ETA. Please stay safe and
> [specific safety instruction if applicable — e.g., do not
> use the stove, stay away from the affected area].

---

**Follow-up text (send immediately after the call):**

> Hi [Tenant Name], confirming we received your emergency report
> for [issue] at [Address]. We are contacting our vendor now
> and will have an ETA for you within [30–60 minutes].
> Call us at [Phone] if anything changes. — [Your Name],
> [Company Name]

---

**Follow-up email:**

**Subject:** Emergency Maintenance — Immediate Action — [Address]

> Hi [Tenant Name],
>
> This confirms we have received your emergency maintenance
> report regarding [issue] at [Property Address, Unit #].
>
> We have immediately escalated this to our emergency vendor
> and are coordinating a same-day response.
>
> **Current status:** Vendor being contacted — ETA to follow
> **Work order:** [Number]
> **Your contact for this emergency:** [Name] at [Phone]
>
> We will update you with a confirmed arrival time within
> [30–60 minutes]. Please do not hesitate to call us directly
> if the situation changes.
>
> [Your Name]
> [Title]
> [Company Name] — Property Management
> [Phone] | [Email]

## Template 3 — Vendor Scheduled Notification

**Send when:** Vendor appointment is confirmed
**Channel:** Email and text

---

**Email version:**

**Subject:** Maintenance Appointment Scheduled — [Address, Unit #]
— [Date]

> Hi [Tenant Name],
>
> We have scheduled a vendor to address your maintenance request
> regarding [issue] at [Property Address, Unit #].
>
> **Appointment details:**
> - Date: [Day, Date]
> - Arrival window: [Time range — e.g., 10:00 AM – 12:00 PM]
> - Vendor: [Company name — or "our maintenance technician"]
> - Work to be done: [Brief description]
> - Work order number: [Number]
>
> **Access:**
> [Choose one:]
> - Please ensure you or an authorized adult is home during
>   this window to provide access.
> - Our technician will access the unit using the lockbox.
>   You do not need to be present.
>
> **If you need to reschedule:**
> Please contact us at [Phone / Email] at least 24 hours before
> the appointment so we can coordinate with the vendor.
>
> Thank you for your patience while we get this resolved.
>
> [Your Name]
> [Title]
> [Company Name] — Property Management
> [Phone] | [Email]

---

**Text version:**

> Hi [Tenant Name], your maintenance appointment for [issue]
> at [Address] is confirmed for [Date] between [Time window].
> Vendor: [Name or company]. Please ensure access is available.
> To reschedule call [Phone] at least 24 hrs in advance.
> — [Your Name], [Company Name]

## Template 4 — Repair Delay Notification

**Send when:** Scheduled repair cannot proceed as planned
**Channel:** Email and text — send immediately upon learning of delay

---

**Email version:**

**Subject:** Maintenance Update — Delay Notice — [Address, Unit #]

> Hi [Tenant Name],
>
> I am writing to update you on the maintenance request for
> [issue] at [Property Address, Unit #] — work order [Number].
>
> Unfortunately we have encountered a delay:
> **Reason for delay:** [Brief explanation — e.g., vendor
> unavailable, part on order, access issue]
>
> **New estimated timeline:** [Revised date or window]
>
> We understand this is an inconvenience and apologize for
> the delay. We are working to resolve this as quickly as
> possible.
>
> [If the issue affects habitability:]
> Because this issue affects your comfort and use of the unit,
> we are treating it as a priority and will update you by
> [specific date] with a confirmed new appointment.
>
> If you have any questions or concerns, please contact us
> directly at [Phone / Email].
>
> [Your Name]
> [Title]
> [Company Name] — Property Management
> [Phone] | [Email]

---

**Text version:**

> Hi [Tenant Name], update on your maintenance request [#]
> for [issue] at [Address] — there is a delay due to
> [brief reason]. New estimated timeline: [Date/window].
> We apologize for the inconvenience. Call [Phone] with
> questions. — [Your Name], [Company Name]

## Template 5 — Entry Notice (24-Hour Notice)

**Send minimum:** 24 hours before entry
**Channel:** Email and text — both required for documentation

---

**Email version:**

**Subject:** Notice of Entry — [Property Address, Unit #] — [Date]

> Dear [Tenant Name],
>
> This notice is to inform you that an authorized representative
> of [Company Name] will be entering your unit for the following
> purpose:
>
> **Property address:** [Full address, Unit #]
> **Date of entry:** [Date]
> **Time of entry:** [Window — e.g., between 10:00 AM and 12:00 PM]
> **Purpose:** [Maintenance repair / Inspection / Vendor access]
> **Who will enter:** [Vendor name or maintenance technician]
>
> You are welcome but not required to be present during the entry.
>
> This notice is provided in accordance with your lease agreement
> and applicable state law requiring [X] hours advance notice
> before entry.
>
> If you have any concerns or need to discuss the timing, please
> contact us at [Phone / Email] as soon as possible.
>
> [Your Name]
> [Title]
> [Company Name] — Property Management
> [Phone] | [Email]

---

**Text version:**

> Hi [Tenant Name], this is your 24-hour notice that
> [Vendor / our technician] will enter your unit at [Address]
> on [Date] between [Time window] to [brief purpose].
> You do not need to be present. Contact [Phone] with any
> questions. — [Your Name], [Company Name]

## Template 6 — Repair Completed Confirmation

**Send when:** Vendor confirms repair is complete
**Channel:** Text first — follow up with email

---

**Text version:**

> Hi [Tenant Name], our technician has completed the repair
> for [issue] at your unit. Please check and let us know if
> everything looks good or if there is anything still
> needing attention. — [Your Name], [Company Name]

---

**Email version:**

**Subject:** Maintenance Complete — [Issue] — [Address, Unit #]

> Hi [Tenant Name],
>
> We wanted to follow up to let you know that the maintenance
> work at your unit has been completed.
>
> **Work order:** [Number]
> **Issue:** [Description]
> **Date completed:** [Date]
> **Work performed:** [Brief description of what was done]
>
> Please take a moment to check the repaired area and let us
> know if:
> - The issue has been fully resolved
> - There is anything that still needs attention
> - You have any concerns about the work performed
>
> Your feedback helps us ensure everything is working properly.
> Please respond to this message or call us at [Phone] by
> [Date — give 2–3 days] so we can officially close this
> work order.
>
> Thank you for your patience throughout this process.
>
> [Your Name]
> [Title]
> [Company Name] — Property Management
> [Phone] | [Email]

## Template 7 — Follow-Up — Issue Not Resolved

**Send when:** Tenant reports repair did not fix the problem
**Channel:** Email and phone call

---

**Email version:**

**Subject:** Maintenance Follow-Up — [Issue] — [Address, Unit #]

> Hi [Tenant Name],
>
> Thank you for letting us know that the issue with [problem]
> at your unit has not been fully resolved. We take this
> seriously and want to make sure this is properly fixed.
>
> **Work order:** [Original number]
> **Original repair date:** [Date]
> **Issue reported unresolved:** [Date]
>
> We are scheduling a follow-up visit and will contact you
> within [24–48 hours] with a confirmed appointment.
>
> In the meantime, please document any ongoing symptoms —
> photos or videos are very helpful for our technician.
>
> We apologize that this was not resolved on the first visit
> and will prioritize getting it fully corrected.
>
> [Your Name]
> [Title]
> [Company Name] — Property Management
> [Phone] | [Email]

## Template 8 — Work Order Close-Out

**Send when:** Issue confirmed fully resolved by tenant
**Channel:** Email

---

**Subject:** Maintenance Resolved — Work Order [#] Closed
— [Address, Unit #]

> Hi [Tenant Name],
>
> Thank you for confirming that the maintenance issue at your
> unit has been resolved. We are officially closing work
> order [Number].
>
> **Summary:**
> - Issue: [Description]
> - Date reported: [Date]
> - Date resolved: [Date]
> - Work order: [Number] — Closed
>
> We appreciate your patience and cooperation throughout
> this process. If any related issues arise in the future
> please do not hesitate to contact us.
>
> [Your Name]
> [Title]
> [Company Name] — Property Management
> [Phone] | [Email]

## Template 9 — Repair Outside Scope — Tenant Responsibility

**Send when:** Requested repair is determined to be tenant's
responsibility under the lease
**Channel:** Email — document carefully

---

**Subject:** Maintenance Request Update — [Address, Unit #]
— [Work Order #]

> Dear [Tenant Name],
>
> Thank you for submitting your maintenance request regarding
> [issue] at [Property Address, Unit #].
>
> After reviewing your request, we have determined that this
> repair falls under tenant responsibility per Section [X]
> of your lease agreement, which states:
>
> [Quote or paraphrase the relevant lease language]
>
> As a result, the cost of this repair would be the
> responsibility of the tenant.
>
> **Your options:**
>
> Option 1: Arrange and pay for the repair yourself using
> a licensed contractor. Please provide us with the contractor's
> name and license number before any work begins, and share
> a copy of the completed work receipt for our records.
>
> Option 2: We can coordinate the repair on your behalf and
> bill the cost to your account. Estimated cost: $[Amount].
> Please confirm in writing if you would like us to proceed
> on this basis.
>
> Please respond by [Date] with your preferred option so we
> can update the work order accordingly.
>
> If you believe this determination is incorrect and would
> like to discuss further, please contact us at [Phone / Email].
>
> [Your Name]
> [Title]
> [Company Name] — Property Management
> [Phone] | [Email]

## Template 10 — Preventive Maintenance Notice

**Send when:** Scheduled routine or preventive maintenance is planned
**Channel:** Email and text

---

**Email version:**

**Subject:** Scheduled Maintenance Notice — [Address, Unit #]
— [Date]

> Dear [Tenant Name],
>
> As part of our routine property maintenance program, we have
> scheduled the following preventive maintenance at your property:
>
> **Work to be performed:** [Description — e.g., HVAC filter
> replacement, annual plumbing inspection, smoke detector check]
> **Date:** [Date]
> **Time window:** [Window]
> **Who will perform the work:** [Vendor / maintenance technician]
> **Estimated duration:** [X minutes / hours]
>
> [If entry required:]
> A technician will need to enter your unit for this work.
> This notice serves as your required [X]-hour advance notice
> of entry per your lease agreement and applicable state law.
>
> You are welcome but not required to be present. If the
> scheduled time does not work for you, please contact us
> at [Phone / Email] at least 48 hours before the scheduled
> date to arrange an alternative time.
>
> This is routine maintenance and does not indicate any
> current problem at your unit.
>
> Thank you for your cooperation.
>
> [Your Name]
> [Title]
> [Company Name] — Property Management
> [Phone] | [Email]

## Communication Timeline Standards

| Template | Send within |
|---|---|
| Initial acknowledgment | 1 business day of request |
| Emergency acknowledgment | 30 minutes of request |
| Vendor scheduled notification | Same day appointment is confirmed |
| Delay notification | Immediately upon learning of delay |
| Entry notice | Minimum 24 hours before entry |
| Repair completed confirmation | Same day repair is confirmed complete |
| Follow-up — unresolved | Within 24 hours of tenant reporting issue |
| Work order close-out | Within 1 business day of tenant confirmation |
| Tenant responsibility notice | Within 2 business days of determination |
| Preventive maintenance notice | Minimum 24–48 hours before work |

---

## Documentation Checklist

For every maintenance request retain:

- [ ] Original request with date and time received
- [ ] Acknowledgment sent — date and method
- [ ] Vendor scheduling confirmation
- [ ] Entry notices sent — date and method
- [ ] All delay notifications
- [ ] Repair completion confirmation from vendor
- [ ] Tenant confirmation of resolution
- [ ] Work order close-out communication
- [ ] Any photos or videos related to the repair
