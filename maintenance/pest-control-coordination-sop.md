# Pest Control Coordination SOP

**Document type:** Standard Operating Procedure
**Category:** Maintenance Operations — Pest Control
**Applies to:** Property managers, operations coordinators, and maintenance
support staff managing pest control for U.S. residential properties
**Last updated:** May 2026
**Author:** Vicky | Property Management Operations

---

## Purpose

This SOP establishes a consistent process for receiving, assessing,
coordinating, and closing pest control requests across managed residential
properties. Pest issues are time-sensitive — they affect tenant health,
property condition, and landlord liability. Delayed or inconsistent
responses create habitability complaints, lease disputes, and potential
legal exposure.

This SOP ensures every pest report is handled with urgency, proper
vendor coordination, and complete documentation.

---

## Scope

Applies to all pest control requests and preventive treatments across
managed residential properties including single-family homes, condominiums,
and multi-unit buildings.

---

## Legal Context

In most U.S. states, landlords are legally required to provide and
maintain pest-free rental units as part of the implied warranty of
habitability. Failure to address pest infestations — particularly
rodents, cockroaches, and bedbugs — can expose the property owner to:

- Rent withholding by the tenant
- Lease termination by the tenant
- Code enforcement violations
- Legal action and damages

Always treat pest reports as habitability matters, not routine
maintenance. Response speed and documentation both matter legally.

> Verify your state's specific landlord obligations for pest control
> before establishing your response standards. Some states place
> responsibility on tenants for certain pest issues caused by
> tenant behavior or negligence.

## Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Infestation** | A pest presence significant enough to affect habitability or tenant health |
| **Isolated incident** | A single pest sighting unlikely to indicate a broader infestation |
| **Treatment** | A professional pest control application targeting a specific pest |
| **Preventive treatment** | A scheduled application designed to prevent pest activity before it begins |
| **Re-inspection** | A follow-up visit to confirm treatment effectiveness |
| **IPM** | Integrated Pest Management — a method combining prevention, monitoring, and targeted treatment |
| **PCO** | Pest Control Operator — a licensed pest control professional |

## Pest Classification and Response Levels

Not all pest reports require the same urgency. Classify correctly
at intake to prioritize response.

### Level 1 — Emergency (respond within 24 hours)

Pests that pose immediate health, safety, or habitability risk:

- Rodents (mice or rats) — active infestation
- Cockroaches — heavy infestation affecting kitchen or multiple rooms
- Bedbugs — confirmed or strongly suspected
- Wasps or hornets — active nest inside or immediately adjacent to unit
- Bees — active swarm inside unit
- Fleas — heavy infestation (often post-pet-removal)

### Level 2 — Urgent (respond within 48–72 hours)

Pests causing significant tenant discomfort or early infestation signs:

- Cockroaches — isolated sightings
- Ants — significant trail or nest identified indoors
- Spiders — large numbers or confirmed dangerous species
- Silverfish — significant presence in multiple areas
- Fruit flies or drain flies — persistent infestation

### Level 3 — Routine (respond within 5–7 business days)

Occasional pest sightings unlikely to indicate infestation:

- Single rodent sighting with no additional evidence
- Occasional ant activity near entry points
- Single spider or insect sighting
- Outdoor pest activity not affecting interior

### Preventive Treatment

Scheduled regardless of tenant complaints — typically quarterly
or semi-annually depending on property location and history.

## Step-by-Step Process

### Step 1 — Receive and log the report

When a tenant reports a pest issue:

- [ ] Log date and time of report
- [ ] Record tenant name, unit address, and contact information
- [ ] Record pest type as described by tenant
- [ ] Record location within unit — kitchen, bedroom, bathroom, etc.
- [ ] Record duration — how long has tenant been seeing the pest?
- [ ] Request photos or videos if not already provided
- [ ] Assign classification level — Emergency / Urgent / Routine

**Work order entry fields:**

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Work order number | |
| Tenant name | |
| Unit address | |
| Pest type reported | |
| Location in unit | |
| Duration of issue | |
| Photos received | Yes / No |
| Classification level | Emergency / Urgent / Routine |
| Date and time logged | |

---

### Step 2 — Send tenant acknowledgment

Send acknowledgment within the timeframe for the classification level:

| Level | Acknowledgment deadline |
|---|---|
| Emergency | Within 2 hours |
| Urgent | Within 24 hours |
| Routine | Within 1 business day |

**Tenant acknowledgment template:**

> Hi [Tenant Name],
>
> Thank you for reporting the pest issue at your unit at
> [Property Address, Unit #]. We have logged your report and
> are coordinating with our pest control team.
>
> A licensed pest control operator will contact you to schedule
> a visit within [timeframe based on level].
>
> In the meantime:
> - Please do not apply your own pesticides or traps without
>   consulting us first — this can interfere with professional
>   treatment
> - Keep the affected area as clear and accessible as possible
> - If you see additional pest activity, please document with
>   photos and send to us
>
> We take pest reports seriously and will keep you updated
> throughout the process.
>
> [Your Name]
> [Company Name] — Property Management

---

### Step 3 — Assess scope before dispatching vendor

For Level 1 and Level 2 reports, gather additional information
before dispatching to ensure the vendor is properly briefed.

**Assessment questions to ask the tenant:**

- How many pests have you seen and over what time period?
- Where exactly in the unit are you seeing them?
- Have you seen any entry points — gaps, holes, cracks?
- Have you noticed any droppings, nests, or damage?
- Have you applied any treatments yourself?
- Are neighboring units reporting the same issue?

**For multi-unit buildings — check adjacent units:**

If a Level 1 pest is reported in a multi-unit building, contact
adjacent units (above, below, and beside) to determine if the
infestation has spread. Do not wait for additional complaints.

- [ ] Adjacent units contacted — Yes / No / Not applicable
- [ ] Adjacent unit reports documented

---

### Step 4 — Select and dispatch pest control operator

Use only licensed PCOs from your approved vendor list.

**PCO selection checklist:**

- [ ] PCO is licensed in the state where the property is located
- [ ] PCO carries general liability insurance — minimum $1M
- [ ] PCO has experience with the specific pest type reported
- [ ] PCO can respond within the required timeframe for the level
- [ ] PCO cost estimate is within the NTE threshold or owner has
      been notified for approval

**Dispatch information to provide PCO:**

- Full property address and unit number
- Pest type and classification level
- Tenant contact name and phone number
- Access instructions — lockbox, key, tenant let-in
- Any known entry points or problem areas
- Whether adjacent units also need inspection
- Any previous treatment history at this property

**Tenant notification of scheduled visit:**

> Hi [Tenant Name],
>
> We have scheduled a pest control visit for your unit at
> [Property Address, Unit #].
>
> Date: [Date]
> Time window: [Arrival window — e.g., 10:00 AM – 12:00 PM]
> Technician: [PCO company name]
>
> To prepare for the visit:
> [Provide preparation instructions from PCO — see Step 5]
>
> Please ensure access is available during this window or
> contact us immediately if you need to reschedule.
>
> [Your Name]
> [Company Name] — Property Management

---

### Step 5 — Provide tenant preparation instructions

Proper tenant preparation is critical for treatment effectiveness.
Instructions vary by pest type — confirm with your PCO before sending.

**General preparation instructions:**

> Before the pest control technician arrives, please:
>
> - Remove all items from under sinks and inside cabinets in
>   the kitchen and bathrooms
> - Clear a 12-inch perimeter around all baseboards in affected rooms
> - Wash and store all dishes and food items in sealed containers
> - Remove pet food and water bowls from the treatment area
> - Make arrangements for pets and children to be out of the unit
>   during treatment and for [X hours] after — confirm with PCO
> - Vacuum all carpeted areas thoroughly before the visit
> - Bag and remove all trash from the unit

**Bedbug-specific preparation:**

> For bedbug treatment, additional preparation is required:
>
> - Wash all bedding, linens, and clothing in hot water (120°F+)
>   and dry on high heat for at least 30 minutes
> - Seal washed items in plastic bags until after treatment
> - Remove all items from closet floors
> - Pull all furniture at least 12 inches from walls
> - Do not move items between rooms — this can spread bedbugs
>   to unaffected areas

**Rodent-specific preparation:**

> For rodent treatment:
>
> - Store all food in sealed hard containers — not bags or boxes
> - Remove clutter from floors, closets, and storage areas
> - Identify and report any gaps, holes, or cracks you can see
>   in walls, floors, or around pipes

---

### Step 6 — Confirm day-of visit

On the day of the scheduled visit:

- [ ] Confirm PCO is en route or on-site at the start of the
      appointment window
- [ ] Send tenant a reminder if not already done
- [ ] If PCO is delayed, notify tenant immediately with revised ETA
- [ ] If PCO cannot attend, reschedule within 24 hours and notify
      tenant immediately

---

### Step 7 — Post-treatment documentation

After treatment is complete, collect all of the following from the PCO:

- [ ] Written treatment report including:
  - Pest type confirmed
  - Severity assessment — isolated / moderate / severe infestation
  - Treatment method and products used
  - Areas treated
  - Entry points identified and sealed (if applicable)
  - Follow-up recommendations
- [ ] Before and after photos where applicable
- [ ] Re-inspection date if required

**Follow-up notification to tenant:**

> Hi [Tenant Name],
>
> Our pest control technician has completed the treatment at
> your unit on [Date].
>
> Treatment summary:
> - Pest: [Type]
> - Areas treated: [List]
> - Products used: [If required to disclose per state law]
>
> Post-treatment instructions:
> [Include PCO's specific post-treatment guidance — e.g., do not
> clean treated surfaces for X hours, ventilate the unit, etc.]
>
> A follow-up inspection is scheduled for [Date] to confirm the
> treatment was effective.
>
> Please contact us immediately if you continue to see pest
> activity before your follow-up date.
>
> [Your Name]
> [Company Name] — Property Management

---

### Step 8 — Re-inspection and confirmation

Schedule a re-inspection 7–14 days after initial treatment
for all Level 1 and Level 2 cases.

- [ ] Re-inspection scheduled with PCO
- [ ] Tenant notified of re-inspection date
- [ ] Re-inspection completed
- [ ] PCO confirms treatment was effective — or recommends
      additional treatment
- [ ] If additional treatment required — repeat Steps 4–8
- [ ] Tenant confirms issue is resolved

**Do not close the work order until:**
- PCO has confirmed treatment effectiveness
- Tenant has confirmed the pest issue is resolved
- All re-inspections are complete

---

### Step 9 — Close the work order

Close only when all items below are confirmed:

- [ ] Treatment completed and verified effective
- [ ] Re-inspection completed (Level 1 and Level 2)
- [ ] Tenant confirmed issue resolved
- [ ] PCO invoice verified and submitted for payment
- [ ] All treatment reports and photos attached to work order
- [ ] Entry points sealed or repair work order created if needed
- [ ] Work order status updated: Closed with date

## Preventive Treatment Program

A scheduled preventive program reduces reactive pest calls
and demonstrates proactive property management to owners and tenants.

**Recommended preventive schedule:**

| Property type | Frequency | Recommended timing |
|---|---|---|
| Single-family home | Quarterly | Jan / Apr / Jul / Oct |
| Multi-unit building | Quarterly — common areas | Jan / Apr / Jul / Oct |
| Multi-unit building | Semi-annual — individual units | Apr / Oct |
| High-risk properties | Monthly — common areas | First week of each month |

**High-risk indicators:**
- History of repeated infestations
- Older building with known structural gaps
- Properties in areas with high rodent or cockroach prevalence
- Properties near restaurants, commercial kitchens, or dumpsters

**Preventive treatment tenant notice:**

> Dear [Tenant Name],
>
> As part of our routine property maintenance program, we have
> scheduled a preventive pest control treatment for your building.
>
> Date: [Date]
> Time: [Window]
> Scope: [Common areas only / Individual units / Both]
>
> [If entering unit:]
> A technician will need access to your unit for approximately
> [X minutes]. Please provide the preparation steps below
> [X days] before the scheduled date.
>
> [Insert relevant preparation instructions]
>
> This treatment is a standard preventive measure and does not
> indicate any current pest problem at your property.
>
> [Your Name]
> [Company Name] — Property Management

## Tenant Responsibility vs. Landlord Responsibility

Document the cause of every infestation — it affects who bears
the cost of treatment.

| Situation | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Infestation present at move-in | Landlord |
| Infestation caused by tenant sanitation issues | Tenant |
| Infestation caused by tenant's pets | Tenant |
| Infestation caused by structural gaps or building issues | Landlord |
| Bedbug infestation — cause unclear | Landlord (in most states) |
| Infestation spread from common areas or adjacent units | Landlord |

> Verify your state's specific rules on tenant vs. landlord pest
> control responsibility. Some states require landlords to cover
> all pest control costs regardless of cause. Others allow landlords
> to charge tenants for infestations they caused. Never charge a
> tenant for pest control without confirming it is legally permitted
> in your state.

## Escalation Triggers

Escalate to property owner immediately if:

- Level 1 pest confirmed — rodents, bedbugs, or heavy cockroach
  infestation
- Infestation affects multiple units in a building
- Tenant threatens to withhold rent or contact code enforcement
- Treatment cost exceeds the NTE threshold
- PCO identifies structural issues enabling pest entry
- Second treatment has not resolved the infestation

## Record Keeping Requirements

Retain all pest control records for a minimum of 3 years:

- [ ] Original work order with timestamps
- [ ] All tenant communications related to the report
- [ ] PCO treatment reports for every visit
- [ ] Before and after photos
- [ ] Re-inspection reports
- [ ] Invoice and payment records
- [ ] Owner notification records (for Level 1 cases)
